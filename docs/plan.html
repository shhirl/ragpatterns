Seven RAGs, one shelf · build plan · 9 September 2026

The Librarian: seven RAG patterns on Shirley's own bookshelf

One corpus: the Goodreads export, the Kindle highlights, a short list of who recommended what, and a few photographed pages. Six fixed questions. Seven retrieval designs behind one interface. The site shows what each design retrieved, what it answered, and whether it was right, with numbers replacing predictions version by version.

What it isA chatbot over Shirley's reading life with a trace panel, and a comparison page where the same six questions run through all seven RAG patterns.
Why booksShe can grade every answer in a second. The data exports in an afternoon. "It invented a book I never read" is the most legible RAG failure there is.
Done whenAll six questions answered by all seven pipelines with measured verdicts, cost and latency on the site, and the "predicted" labels removed.

Privacy and publishing rules

Highlights only, never full text. A highlight is a short quotation with attribution, which is fine to publish in small numbers; a book is not.

Recommenders appear as initials. The mapping from initials to people lives in the CSV on her machine, not in the repo.

Shirley curates the export before ingest. Any title she does not want on a public page is deleted from the CSV, and the corpus is whatever is left. The site says the corpus is curated.

3The question set: six questions, one per failure mode

Each question is a diagnostic. Q1 is a control every pattern should pass. Q2 is new relative to the handstand version: structured facts, the most common real-world RAG failure. Exact wording and the real books get fixed after the export is inspected; the shape of each question does not change.

The prediction I most expect to be wrong: naive on Q1. Kindle highlights are short, so the nearest chunk is often a different book quoting the same author. If that happens, Q1 stops being a control and reranking earns its call one column earlier.

4Seven pipelines are compositions of five retrievers

There are not seven systems. There are five retrievers and one generation call, and each pattern is a rule for which retrievers run and in what order. Building it that way is what makes the comparison fair: the same reranker, the same graph, the same model, in every column.

One interface

answer(question: str, pattern: str | None, image: bytes | None) -> {
    "answer":   str,
    "context":  [ {source, id, score, stage} ],   # what reached the model, in order
    "route":    str,                              # which pattern ran (router's choice or forced)
    "calls":    int,                              # model calls, counted by the code
    "ms":       int,
    "usage":    {input_tokens, output_tokens, usd},
}

The eval, the site's live demo, and the trace panel all call this one function. What is measured is what a visitor gets.

6 questions × 7 patterns × 5 runs is 210 answers per version. Shirley reads every one before any number is published; the invented-book count is the check that catches what she misses. A second reader grades a sample before v5 and the agreement rate is published as the ceiling, as on fixmybanana.

6Versions: what turns from prediction into measurement

v0

Site rewritten for books. Thesis, corpus, six question shapes, predicted traces per cell, scorecard, decision guide, method page. Labels say "predicted".

v1

Ingest and the first three retrievers. Parse both exports, build SQLite, embed highlights, add the cross-encoder and the SQL tool. Naive and rerank rows measured; Q2 gets its first real answer through a forced SQL path so the number exists early. Done when rows 1 and 2 of the grid carry measured cells with cost and latency.

v2

Graph and hybrid. Claude extraction over highlights and reviews with the fixed schema; NetworkX traversal; the extracted graph committed as JSON. Done when the Q4 column is measured and the graph is browsable on the site.

v3

Multimodal. Voyage multimodal embeddings for the photos, vision generation. Done when Q5 is measured with the photographed pages.

v4

Router and multi-agent. Haiku 4.5 router over the five retrievers; planner, three sub-agents, synthesizer. Done when Q6 and the cost column are measured.

v5

The numbers. All 210 answers graded, consistency and invented-book counts published, second reader's agreement, ten more questions from how people actually ask, the live "ask the librarian" box with the trace panel. Done when no cell on the site is a prediction.

9Decisions that are Shirley's

Retire the handstand v0 entirely, or keep it as a second corpus later? Recommendation: retire it now, keep the renderer. The site tells one story.
Recommendations file. Write recommendations.csv by hand (twenty rows is plenty), or tag Goodreads shelves like rec-ab and re-export? Recommendation: the CSV; shelves get messy.
Which books for Q3 and Q5. One book she rated low that the crowd rated high, and three to five pages she is happy to photograph. Chosen after the export is inspected.
Domain. ragpatterns.com is parked at Cloudflare checkout at $10.46 a year. The corpus change does not affect it. Buy, or close.
Abandoned books. Q6 needs them. Are they on a Goodreads shelf (e.g. "dnf") or only in her head? If the latter, five titles in a column of the CSV is enough.
